The week

Three full-body strength sessions hit back, chest, arms, legs and core every time, so each muscle is trained three times a week. Saturday builds stamina. The rest days are when you actually grow, so keep them.

Start here

  1. Open Training and set a level for each exercise. If you are unsure, choose Easy; you will move up within weeks.
  2. Open Plan, tap a day in October or November, and pick one of the three options in each of the five slots. The yellow bar adds up your calories and protein.
  3. Want to cook something specific? Recipes has all 300 dishes sorted by cuisine and meal, plus the sauces.
  4. If 3,000 calories is a lot more than you eat now, build up: about 2,500 in week one, 2,700 in week two, then the full amount.
  5. On Sunday, follow the two-hour cook on the Prep tab. That single session is what makes weekdays easy.
  6. Weigh yourself three mornings a week and log your reps every session. Those two numbers tell you whether the plan is working.

A quick health note. At 5 ft 8 and 51 kg your BMI is about 17, which is below the healthy range, so gaining weight is exactly the right goal. If you have always struggled to put weight on despite eating plenty, or the scales have not moved after four weeks of hitting these numbers, mention it to your GP so they can rule out anything underlying.

Calorie figures are estimated for a 51 kg, 173 cm man in his early twenties training four times a week (maintenance roughly 2,300 to 2,400 kcal). Recipe calories and protein are careful estimates, not lab values; brands vary.

Training

One bar is enough. Pulling happens on the bar, pushing happens on the ground, legs are trained one at a time so your bodyweight is heavy enough, and a backpack becomes your weight stack later on.

How muscle gets built with nothing but a bar

  1. Train every muscle two or three times a week. All three strength sessions are full-body, which suits a beginner better than splitting body parts across days.
  2. Pick the level that fits the rep range. If you can't reach the lower number with clean form, drop a level. When you beat the top number on every set, move up.
  3. Stop one or two reps before failure. The last reps should be slow and hard but still look like the first one.
  4. Lower slowly. Two to three seconds down on everything. The lowering half builds as much muscle as the lifting half.
  5. Rest properly. Two minutes on main lifts, about a minute inside supersets. Rushing turns strength work into cardio.
  6. Write it down. Note your reps for every set on your phone and beat one number each session. That is progressive overload.
  7. Build stamina without burning your gains. One short, hard session a week plus daily walking raises fitness. Long runs would eat the calorie surplus you are working to create.

When to train

Best slot: 4 to 6:30 pm

  • Body temperature, joint mobility and strength all peak in the late afternoon, and you will have three meals in you.
  • The same slot every time matters more than which slot. Your body learns to perform at the hour you usually train.
  • Eat the light meal 60 to 90 minutes before. Eat dinner within about two hours after.
  • Never train fasted. At your weight there is nothing spare to run on.

When the evenings go dark

  • Until late October there is daylight until at least 5:45 pm.
  • The clocks go back on 25 October 2026. Sunset drops to before 5 pm and reaches about 4 pm in December.
  • From November to February, train between 11 am and 2 pm on days your timetable allows, or first thing after breakfast. Avoid unlit parks after dark.
  • Morning session? Eat at least half your breakfast an hour before, and add three or four minutes to the warm-up because joints are stiffer early.

What to bring

  • Water, and a small towel: it is your rowing handle and it dries a wet bar.
  • Your phone, for the rest timer and your rep log.
  • A backpack. Empty now; books and water bottles turn it into weights later.
  • In winter: thin grippy gloves and layers you can peel off. Extend the warm-up below 10°C.
  • Worth buying later: a long resistance band for assisted pull-ups, and liquid chalk.

      Set every exercise to

      The first 12 weeks

      1. Weeks 1 and 2: learn. Choose levels where you can hit the lower rep number with clean form. Stop two or three reps before failure and do three sets where four are listed. Expect to be sore for a couple of days after the first sessions; it fades by week two. Build your food up towards the target.
      2. Weeks 3 to 6: build. Full sets, one or two reps in reserve. Add a rep somewhere every session. Move up a level on any exercise where you reach the top of the range on every set.
      3. Week 7: deload. Same sessions, half the sets, nothing close to failure. Keep eating the same. You will come back stronger.
      4. Weeks 8 to 12: push. Take the last set of each exercise to the point where form would break. Start loading the backpack in 2 to 3 kg steps. Add a fourth round to the supersets if you are recovering well.
      5. Week 13: retest. Deload again, then test your best set of pull-ups and push-ups and compare your weight, photos and tape measurements with week one.

      When something isn't working

      I can't do a single pull-up

      Most people can't when they start. Stay on Easy: hangs build your grip, scapular pulls teach your back to switch on, and slow negatives build exactly the strength a pull-up needs. Three sessions a week of that gets most people their first chin-up in three to six weeks, faster as you gain weight in muscle rather than lose it.

      The scales aren't moving

      Weigh yourself three mornings a week, after the toilet and before eating, and use the average. If it has risen less than ¼ kg a week for two weeks, add about 250 kcal a day: a glass of whole milk and a handful of nuts does it. Check you are really eating all five meals; skipped late meals are the usual culprit.

      I'm never hungry enough
      • Drink some calories: the shakes in the menu are 450 to 800 kcal and barely touch your appetite.
      • Eat by the clock, not by hunger. Appetite catches up within two or three weeks.
      • Add dense extras rather than bigger portions: a spoon of olive oil over rice or pasta, butter on rotis, peanut butter, cheese, dates.
      • Save water for between meals rather than just before them.
      • If you are too full to finish a meal, leave some of the meat rather than the rice, bread or potatoes. You are already well over your protein need; it is the calories you are short of.
      I've been stuck on the same reps for three sessions

      Check food and sleep first; nine times out of ten that is the cause. Then try rest-pause on the last set: do your reps, rest 15 to 20 seconds, and squeeze out two or three more. If everything feels heavy for a week, take a deload week early.

      Something hurts

      Muscle burn and next-day soreness are normal. Sharp or pinching pain in a joint is not: stop that exercise, drop a level, and check your form against the cues. The most common beginner problem with bar work is sore elbows from too much pulling too soon, which is why this plan ramps pulling gradually. Pain that lasts more than a week or two deserves a physio or GP.

      It's raining, freezing or dark

      Dry the bar with your towel and wear gloves; a wet steel bar is slippery. If the park is out, do that day's push-ups, leg work and floor core at home, skip the bar work, and add one extra set of pulling to your next two sessions.

      Should I take supplements?

      None are required. The one with solid evidence for strength and muscle is creatine monohydrate, 3 to 5 g a day; buy it as plain powder (capsules are often gelatine) and look for a halal-certified brand. The NHS advises everyone in the UK to consider 10 micrograms of vitamin D a day through autumn and winter. Protein powder is only useful if you can't reach 110 g from food, and with this menu you will.

      Meal times, portions and labels

      How much, and how to adjust

      • Aim for about 3,000 kcal a day. If that is far above what you eat now, step up over three weeks (roughly 2,500, then 2,700, then 3,000) by serving yourself smaller portions of the same meals.
      • Very lean people often burn off part of a surplus as heat and fidgeting, so you may need the full 3,300 or so. Your real target is whatever intake moves the scales ¼ to ½ kg a week.
      • Let the scales steer. Under ¼ kg a week for a fortnight: add 250 kcal. Over ¾ kg a week: take 200 off.
      • Protein target is 110 g or more (about 2.2 g per kg). Most combinations here give well over that, so never add protein at the expense of carbs.
      • Drink 2 to 3 litres of water a day, mostly between meals.

      Labels worth a glance

      • Buy halal-certified meat as you normally would; nothing here uses pork, gelatine, wine, mirin or sake.
      • Soy sauce, miso, gochujang, chilli bean and black bean pastes: a few brands list alcohol.
      • Parmesan, Gruyère and manchego are traditionally made with animal rennet. Supermarkets sell vegetarian versions.
      • Stock cubes, gravy granules and dashi powder: choose halal, vegetable or fish-based.
      • Sausages, sucuk, chorizo, rashers and pâté: halal-certified versions.

      Prep and shopping

      Should you meal prep? Yes, but only lunch and dinner. Those are the two meals that fall apart on a busy day. Breakfast, the light meal and the midnight meal stay quick: almost all of them take under ten minutes or no cooking at all.

      Repeat on purpose. The calendar shows three options per slot so every day looks different, but you don't need to cook all of them. In practice, choose two or three recipes tagged Batch each week and eat each of them several times. Any Batch lunch or dinner can stand in for any other lunch or dinner on any day; they are all in the same calorie range by design.

      Cook once: eat one today, fridge one, freeze two. After a month your freezer is a menu of six or seven different home-cooked meals and most days need no cooking at all.

      Sunday: the two-hour cook

      1. 0:00Oven on. Get an oven batch in first: the tandoori traybake, the roast chicken traybake or jerk chicken.
      2. 0:15Start a hob batch: bolognese, chilli, keema, butter chicken or daal.
      3. 0:35Put on rice or pasta if today's boxes need it. Boil six eggs.
      4. 0:50While things simmer: two jars of tiramisu overnight oats, four freezer bags of shake ingredients (mango and oats, or cocoa and oats), and chop onions, chillies and salad veg into tubs.
      5. 1:10As the traybake comes out, one bake goes in: flapjacks, baked oats or cornbread.
      6. 1:30Portion into boxes with the lids off so they cool fast. Spread rice out on a tray to cool it within the hour. Label every box with its name and the date.
      7. 1:50One box of each in the fridge, the rest in the freezer. Wash up.

      Wednesday: 45-minute top-up

      • Cook one more batch recipe; daal and the soups are the quickest.
      • Refill the oat jars and boiled eggs.
      • Move Thursday's and Friday's frozen boxes to the fridge.

      Every night: 2 minutes

      • Move tomorrow's frozen box to the fridge to defrost.
      • Put tomorrow's breakfast jar at the front of the fridge and pack the light meal if you will be out.

      Kit that makes it work

      • 10 to 12 microwave-safe boxes with lids, and 4 jars
      • Freezer bags, masking tape and a marker
      • A large non-stick pan, a big pot, two baking trays
      • A blender, even a cheap one, for the shakes
      • Optional: a rice cooker

      Storage rules

      • Cool cooked food within one to two hours, then refrigerate or freeze it.
      • In the fridge, eat it within 2 days.
      • Rice is stricter: cool it within 1 hour and eat it within 24 hours. Any box containing rice that you won't eat tomorrow goes straight in the freezer.
      • Frozen meals are good for about 3 months. Defrost overnight in the fridge and eat within 24 hours.
      • Reheat once only, until steaming hot all the way through. Stir halfway if you are using a microwave.

      These follow NHS food safety guidance on batch cooking, leftovers and rice.
